Frequently Asked Questions

Short, direct answers with permanent links for easy sharing.

Income Tax

Income Tax

#

Which tax regime is better?

The better regime depends on your income mix and eligible deductions. Compare both using the same income data each year before choosing.

#

Is the new regime mandatory?

No. It is the default regime, but eligible taxpayers may opt for the old regime. Taxpayers with business or professional income face additional switching rules.

#

Can I switch between regimes?

Most taxpayers without business income can generally choose each year. Taxpayers with business or professional income should review Form 10-IEA and switching restrictions.

#

Do I need to file ITR if TDS is deducted?

TDS does not replace filing. You may still need to file based on income, assets, transactions, refund claims, or other statutory conditions.

#

Is bonus taxable?

Yes. Employment bonus is normally taxed as salary in the year it becomes taxable, and the employer may deduct TDS.

#

Are gifts taxable?

Some gifts can be taxable when aggregate value crosses the statutory threshold. Gifts from specified relatives and certain occasions are generally exempt, subject to conditions.

#

Is gratuity taxable?

It depends on the employer type, coverage under gratuity law, amount received, and available exemption limits. Any excess exemption can be taxable as salary.

#

Is pension taxable?

Regular pension is generally salary income. Family pension and commuted pension follow different rules and exemptions.

#

Can I claim HRA?

HRA exemption is generally available under the old regime when eligible salary, rent, and residence conditions are met.

#

Can I claim LTA?

Eligible employees may claim LTA under the old regime for qualifying domestic travel, subject to block-period and documentation rules.

#

How much income is tax-free?

It depends on the regime, residential status, income type, and assessment year. For FY 2025-26, Section 87A can reduce eligible new-regime tax to zero up to the applicable threshold; special-rate income needs separate treatment.

#

What is rebate under Section 87A?

It is a rebate for eligible resident individuals whose total income stays within the prescribed threshold. Limits differ between regimes and assessment years.

#

What is cess?

Health and Education Cess is an additional levy on calculated income tax and surcharge. The commonly applied rate is 4%.

#

What is surcharge?

Surcharge is additional tax for taxpayers whose income exceeds specified high-income thresholds. Marginal relief may apply.

#

What happens if I don't file ITR?

Possible consequences include late fees, interest, loss carry-forward restrictions, notices, and delayed refunds. Available belated or updated-return routes depend on statutory time limits.

Investments

Investments

#

Is SIP guaranteed?

No. SIP is only an investment method; returns depend on the underlying asset and market performance.

#

Is Step-Up SIP better?

It can build a larger corpus by increasing contributions over time, but it is better only when the higher future contributions remain affordable.

#

SIP vs FD?

SIPs can offer market-linked growth with volatility. FDs offer stated interest and capital stability but may deliver lower post-tax growth.

#

SIP vs PPF?

A mutual-fund SIP is market-linked and liquid based on the scheme. PPF is government-backed, long-term, and subject to contribution and withdrawal rules.

#

SIP vs NPS?

SIP is a contribution method available across mutual funds. NPS is a retirement product with regulated allocation, tax rules, and withdrawal restrictions.

#

Which mutual fund should beginners choose?

There is no universal choice. Beginners commonly evaluate diversified, low-cost funds after considering risk capacity, time horizon, and asset allocation.

#

How much should I invest monthly?

Start with the amount left after essential expenses, insurance, and emergency savings. Link the contribution to a specific goal and increase it as income grows.

#

Can I stop SIP anytime?

Most open-ended mutual-fund SIP instructions can be stopped, though scheme exit loads, lock-ins, or platform processing timelines may apply.

#

What happens if I miss a SIP?

The instalment usually fails and no units are purchased. Repeated failures may cancel the mandate and banks may charge return fees.

#

Is SWP taxable?

Each SWP withdrawal is a redemption. The gain portion can be taxable based on fund type, holding period, and applicable capital-gains rules.

#

What is CAGR?

CAGR is the constant annual growth rate that connects a beginning value to an ending value over a period. It assumes no intermediate cash flows.

#

What is XIRR?

XIRR is an annualised return that accounts for the exact dates and amounts of multiple investments and withdrawals.

Capital Gains

Capital Gains

#

How are stocks taxed?

Sale gains are generally classified as short-term or long-term based on holding period. Rates depend on listing, transaction conditions, and current law.

#

How are mutual funds taxed?

Tax treatment depends on the fund asset mix, acquisition date, holding period, and whether the gain is short-term or long-term.

#

What is STCG?

Short-term capital gain is gain from an asset sold within its prescribed short-term holding period.

#

What is LTCG?

Long-term capital gain is gain from an asset held beyond the prescribed holding period. Exemptions and tax rates depend on asset type.

#

When is capital gain calculated?

Capital gain is normally calculated on transfer or sale using consideration minus eligible cost and transfer expenses, with adjustments where law requires.

#

Can I offset losses?

Eligible capital losses may be set off against permitted capital gains. Short-term and long-term losses have different set-off rules.

#

How long can losses be carried forward?

Eligible capital losses can generally be carried forward for up to eight assessment years when the return is filed within the required timeline.

Salary

Salary

#

Why is my in-hand lower than CTC?

CTC can include employer PF, gratuity, insurance, bonus, and other non-monthly components. Payroll then deducts employee PF, tax, and professional tax.

#

Does employer PF count as salary?

Employer PF is usually part of CTC but is not paid as monthly cash. Tax treatment depends on contribution limits and applicable provident-fund rules.

#

Is gratuity part of CTC?

Many employers include a projected gratuity cost in CTC, but payment occurs only when eligibility and service conditions are satisfied.

#

Is HRA compulsory?

No. HRA is a salary-structure component decided by the employer; it is not mandatory for every employee.

#

Can I claim HRA without rent receipts?

You must be able to substantiate rent payment. Employer payroll procedures and tax assessment documentation requirements can differ.

RSUs

RSUs

#

Are RSUs taxed twice?

The same income is not normally taxed twice: vesting value is salary income, while only the later change from vesting cost basis is considered for capital gains.

#

When are RSUs taxed?

RSUs are generally taxed as a salary perquisite when shares vest or are transferred, followed by capital-gains tax when shares are sold.

#

Do I need Schedule FA?

Resident and ordinarily resident taxpayers may need Schedule FA for foreign shares or accounts. Reporting can apply even without a sale.

#

How do I report foreign RSUs?

Report the vesting perquisite in salary and any sale in capital gains. Eligible taxpayers may also need foreign-asset and foreign-income schedules.

#

Is TDS deducted on RSUs?

Employers commonly account for TDS on the taxable vesting perquisite, sometimes by withholding or selling shares. Check payroll and broker statements.

#

What if I don't sell my RSUs?

Vesting tax can still arise even if shares are retained. Future price changes matter when the shares are eventually sold, and foreign-asset reporting may continue.

EPF / PPF / NPS

EPF / PPF / NPS

#

Is EPF taxable?

EPF is generally tax-efficient, but employer contributions, high employee contributions, interest, and early withdrawals can trigger tax under specific rules.

#

Is PPF taxable?

PPF contributions may qualify under Section 80C in the old regime, while interest and eligible maturity proceeds are generally exempt under current rules.

#

Is NPS taxable?

NPS has contribution deductions subject to regime and section limits. Withdrawal, annuity, and employer-contribution treatment follow separate rules.

#

Can I withdraw EPF?

Withdrawals are allowed in specified circumstances such as job separation, retirement, or approved partial-withdrawal purposes, subject to conditions.

#

Can I withdraw PPF early?

PPF permits limited partial withdrawals and certain premature-closure cases after prescribed periods and subject to conditions.

#

Which is better: PPF or EPF?

EPF is employment-linked retirement saving; PPF is voluntary long-term saving. The better fit depends on eligibility, liquidity, contribution capacity, and goals.

#

Can I have multiple PPF accounts?

An individual generally cannot maintain multiple PPF accounts in their own name, apart from permitted guardian arrangements for minors.

Banking

Banking

#

Is FD interest taxable?

Yes. FD interest is generally taxable as income from other sources at the applicable slab rate.

#

Is RD interest taxable?

Yes. RD interest is generally taxable as income from other sources at the applicable slab rate.

#

What is TDS on FD?

Banks may deduct TDS when interest crosses the applicable threshold. TDS is a tax credit, not necessarily the final tax liability.

#

Can I break an FD?

Most callable FDs allow premature closure, often with a reduced interest rate or penalty. Non-callable deposits may not permit it.

#

Are bank deposits insured?

Eligible deposits are insured by DICGC up to the statutory limit per depositor per bank, combining covered balances across branches.

GST

GST

#

How do I calculate GST?

For an exclusive price, GST equals base amount multiplied by the GST rate. Add that tax to the base to get the final amount.

#

How do I remove GST?

For a GST-inclusive amount, divide by one plus the GST rate to find the base, then subtract the base from the total.

#

What is CGST?

CGST is the central component of GST generally charged on an intra-state supply.

#

What is SGST?

SGST is the state component of GST generally charged alongside CGST on an intra-state supply.

#

What is IGST?

IGST generally applies to inter-state supplies and imports, with settlement between the Centre and destination state.

Loans

Loans

#

What is EMI?

EMI is the fixed periodic payment used to repay loan principal and interest over the agreed tenure.

#

How is EMI calculated?

EMI uses the principal, periodic interest rate, and number of instalments under an amortisation formula.

#

What is reducing balance?

Interest is calculated on the outstanding principal, so the interest portion generally falls as repayments reduce the balance.

#

Can I prepay my loan?

Many loans allow part or full prepayment. Check lock-ins, charges, lender procedures, and any tax-benefit implications.

#

Does prepayment reduce EMI?

Depending on lender policy, prepayment may reduce the EMI, shorten the tenure, or allow you to choose between the two.
